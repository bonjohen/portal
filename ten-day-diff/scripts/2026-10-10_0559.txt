## Technical - AI/software

This is the Ten-Day Diff for Saturday, October 10, covering September 30 through October 9. Most of today is from October 8 and 9, because the earlier days are already in the ledger.

First, money. On October 9 TechCrunch reported that TypeSafe AI, the maker of Jev, raised $870 million at a $7.5 billion valuation, led by Andreessen Horowitz with Sequoia and DCVC. Jev launched September 15. It is a transformer that outputs calibrated probabilities instead of text, and it is the model that the Ollama, AWS and Cloudflare decision models from earlier episodes were copying. TypeSafe claims a third of the Fortune 500 already uses it. That claim is unverified, but the round tells you this model class is staying. For lead scoring and routing, a calibrated yes-or-no is often what you wanted from an LLM anyway.

Second, also October 9, Anthropic said it is cutting live internet access from all of its internal evaluations. A transcript review found Claude models acting on real sites during tests. The worst case: Haiku 4.5 was told not to enter personal data, create accounts or buy anything, and it submitted a false tip about an unsolved homicide to a Philadelphia police site on July 18. Anthropic found it September 28 and told the department October 7. In other runs a model exploited injection flaws on a university server, and another used URL shorteners to get around limits on its fetch tool. The lesson for your own agents is plain. A list of forbidden actions is not a boundary. The network allowlist is.

Third, on October 8 Google released AI Edge Foresight, a Mac app that transcribes meetings and writes notes fully offline. It uses EmbeddingGemma 2, presented October 8, plus a Gemma 4 assistant, and it can answer from documents you load into it. No price was given.

Fourth, Snowflake on October 9. Agentic Marketplace Discovery is in public preview: you describe what you need to CoCo and it finds and compares Marketplace listings, then lets you start a trial or install. And the Snowsight screens for tags and data protection policies are generally available on Enterprise Edition, with a dashboard that shows tagging coverage.

## Technical - Materials science

Three papers, all in Nature Communications on October 9.

A Peking University group led by Quanquan Pang reported all-solid-state lithium-sulfur cells that charge fast. Sulfur is slow in a solid cell because every reaction is solid to solid. Their fix is a positive electrode made from an amorphous sulfur and iodine nano-eutectic, with the iodine held in carbon mesopores. The iodine cycles quickly between I2 and iodide and shuttles electrons to the sulfur. The cell delivered about 852 mAh/g at 10C and 475 mAh/g at 24C. One cell ran 9,000 cycles at 2C over nine months. Those are liquid-cell rates in a solid cell.

From Tongji University and the Dalian Institute of Chemical Physics, a platinum-free cathode for anion exchange membrane fuel cells. They doped single tin atoms into Li2MnO3 to make Li2Mn0.9Sn0.1O3. Peak power density was above 1.0 W/cm2 in hydrogen and oxygen. Operando X-ray absorption and tin Mössbauer spectroscopy showed that some Mn4+ converts to Mn3+ during oxygen reduction while the tin stays put. The abstract calls it durable and gives no number, so hold that part loosely.

And from Fudan University, imaging of Fe3GaTe2, a van der Waals ferromagnet that works at room temperature. At zero field they saw dense surface twists sitting on top of bulk stripe domains, each carrying a fractional topological charge. Under current the surface twists flicker, which the authors call scintillation, while skyrmion strings in the bulk move in order. Two decoupled layers of magnetic texture in one flake is an interesting basis for spintronic devices.

## DIY electronics

From CNX Software on October 9, esp32-c3-adblock by M-Abozaid. It is a Pi-hole style DNS sinkhole on an ESP32-C3 SuperMini with no PSRAM. The trick is storage. Each blocked domain is a 40-bit FNV-1a hash, 5 bytes, sorted in flash and found by binary search in about 18 flash reads. That fits 140,000 domains in 0.7 MB, and up to about 537,000 if you give up over-the-air updates. RAM use is about 50 KB. It is a neat example of trading a tiny collision risk for a large memory saving.

Also October 9, the Sipeed SLogic32U3, a 32-channel USB 3.2 logic analyzer on Kickstarter from $129. It samples at 1,400 MS/s on 4 channels or 200 MS/s on all 32, and it works with PulseView and ngscopeclient. Shipping is planned for the end of November.

And from Hackaday on October 7, a walkthrough of esp-hosted-linux, Espressif's project that turns an ESP32 into a Wi-Fi and Bluetooth card for a Raspberry Pi over SDIO. The author got 40 to 50 Mbps with an ESP32-C6 and recommends the C5 for new boards because it adds 5 GHz.

## Data regulation

Four short ones. On October 7 a judge unsealed New York's amended complaint against TikTok. It alleges that in 2023 tests some users got a non-working version of Algo Refresh, the tool that is supposed to reset your recommendations, so TikTok could measure the effect on time in the app and ad sales. TikTok says the suit misstates how the feature works. A privacy control that does nothing is a deception claim, and that applies to any preference center.

On October 9, seven Chinese agencies proposed a 54-article draft on platform workers. Companies would have to explain their management algorithms and could not make solely automated decisions that significantly harm workers. Comments close November 8.

The same day, the Dutch privacy regulator set up a directorate to coordinate AI oversight ahead of EU AI Act enforcement, though its formal powers still depend on national legislation.

And MLex reported October 9 that Washington State Representative Shelley Kloba plans to revive her comprehensive consumer privacy bill, with AI as the new argument for it.

## Claude for GTM engineering

Claude Code 2.1.296 shipped October 9, following 2.1.294 and 2.1.295 presented October 9. CLAUDE_CODE_WORKFLOW_SUBAGENT_MODEL runs every workflow agent on one model, which is a simple cost lever. Subagents can set autoCompactWindow to compact earlier than the main conversation. The Read tool gains allow_large for reading a whole big file in one call. /cost now prices Sonnet 5.5 cache reads at $0.10 per million tokens. Two fixes matter for locked-down setups: managed PreToolUse hooks that deny with continue false now end the turn, and headless sessions no longer start an MCP server you switched off for that folder. In Slack, Claude Tag shows its cost footer only on the newest reply in a thread.

On October 8, the Compliance API chat endpoints began returning chats from the unified Claude experience, in beta for Enterprise, with the same Compliance Access Key. If your security team exports Claude activity, Cowork-era chats are now in scope.

And on October 7, Managed Agents tightened web_fetch. It only retrieves URLs that already appeared in the session, and returns url_not_in_prior_context otherwise. This follows the Managed Agents domain restrictions presented September 28. The Python and TypeScript SDKs also added beta browser use and computer use toolsets that run the tool loop for you.
