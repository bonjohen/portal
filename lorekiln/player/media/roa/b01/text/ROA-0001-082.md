Episode 82: Off the point

At first light on Friday Professor Scott stood at the main gate of the campus with his coat buttoned wrong and Zoe's note in his pocket, and Stelios Rigas would not let him through.

"You were in the hall," Rigas said. "I've got you on the list. Second line from the top, after the giant." He tapped the clipboard. "Nobody who stood near him goes out till the dean says."

"A woman has gone out to sea in a fishing boat. I need to be on the quay."

"Then she's breaking the lockdown, and you can tell her so when she comes back."

Scott could have walked through him. The stick was in his hand, and it would have been nothing to put the man on his back and step over the barrier. He knew exactly how he would do it. He stood still instead and argued, politely, while the sky over the sea went from grey to white, and Rigas rang the dean's office, and the dean's office didn't answer, and Rigas rang it again. It was twenty to eight when a guard came up from the side gate with a message that the dean would see the professor at ten, and Rigas lifted the barrier with a face like a shut door.

The taxi from Kalandra took the coast road north at a speed the driver kept apologising for. Scott didn't hear him. Out to the left the sea lay flat under a low sky, and far up the shore, off the point at Nea Fokea, a column of birds that weren't birds hung over a white hull.

The harbour at Nea Fokea was a curve of quay with a harbour office at the end of it and the old tower standing on the point above. Two fishing boats were tied up. The berth beside them was empty, and a girl of about twelve stood at the edge of it in a yellow oilskin with her hands in her pockets, watching the sea.

"They went at seven," she said, before Scott asked. "My dad and the lady. She paid the diesel." She didn't take her eyes off the water. "It's the rich man's boat. He's called the same as us."

Scott went up into the harbour office. The man inside had a pair of binoculars on a strap and gave them to him without a word, as if he had been waiting for someone to hand them to.

Through them the Anthousa jumped close, long and white, lying across the swell with no way on her. A small blue fishing boat was coming up under her stern. Scott found the figure in the bow by her coat, and watched her go up the yacht's boarding ladder and in over the rail, and the boat stand off to wait. Above the yacht the drones hung in their line, a dozen of them, perhaps more, motionless against the cloud.

Then the first one came down.

It dropped on the yacht's foredeck like a gull onto a scrap and stayed there. The second came down beside it. They came down in turn, not fast, as if each waited for the last to settle, and the fourth went low along the side of the hull and in through a hatch near the stern. Scott knew what the hatch was before the smoke came out of it. Every yacht kept her fuel aft.

The smoke came out black, and then the flame behind it.

The stick moved in his hand.

He felt it the way he always did, the dead weight gone suddenly alive, the shaft warm in his palm and wanting to go. It would have gone, too. From the harbour wall to the point was nothing to it. He could have put it through the line of drones over that hull like a needle through cloth and had it back in his hand before the last of them hit the water.

And he was angry. He had never been so angry in a hundred years. He was angry at the machines and whoever had sent them, and angrier still at Zoe for sitting in the bow of a fishing boat under them, and he could feel the anger in his arm like a second bone. If he threw now, it would not go for the drones. It would go across the water to the one he loved, wherever she stood, and she was standing in its path.

So he stood on the harbour wall with the binoculars and did nothing, and hated it more than anything he had done in his life.

Vardis came up out of the smoke.

He came slowly, an old man in a good coat with one arm across his face, and in the other he had the grey case. Zoe came out behind him, bent double in the smoke. The fishing boat had come in again under the side. He went to the rail above it and lowered the case down to the man at the tiller, and then he took Zoe by the wrist and the elbow, and Scott saw her go over the side into the boat, half into the water and out again, the old man holding her the whole way down.

Then Vardis stopped at the rail.

He stood as if he were listening to something nobody else could hear. Scott saw his head turn a little, one way and then the other, the way a man looks at doors in a corridor. His hand with the ring on it was closed on the rail. Then he shook his head, once, quite clearly, and put his foot against the fishing boat's gunwale and pushed it off.

The fishing boat swung away from the hull, and the man at the tiller opened the throttle.

It came in across the swell with the smoke going up behind it. Scott lost the old man in the smoke and found him again at the rail, and lost him again. Halfway to the quay the Anthousa's stern went under. She lay a long moment with her bow in the air, white against black, and then she slid down off the point and was gone, and the sea closed over the place, and there was only smoke on the water, drifting.

The drones lifted off nothing and hung there.

Scott went down the steps to the quay, and the girl stood beside him without speaking, and the boat came in. Zoe was sitting in the bottom of it with the case in her arms, wet to the waist and alone. Her face was grey. Maria's father threw a line, and Scott caught it, and made it fast with his own hands, and only then noticed that they were shaking.

He gave her his hand and she came up the ladder with the case.

"He kept the receipt," she said. "He gave it back to me. It's in the lid." Her teeth were chattering. "He said the ring was showing him the ways out. Every one of them. He said he'd looked at every one, and they were all good, and somebody else paid for all of them. He said for once he wasn't going to take any." She stopped. "Then he pushed us off."

Scott put his coat round her, over the case and all.

Out over the water, where the smoke was thinning, the drones had not gone home. They hung a while longer above the place where the yacht had been. Then, slowly, one after another, they turned, and came in low toward the quay, toward the fishing boat at its rope and the grey case in Zoe's arms.
