Episode 36: A drone over the pines

On Saturday morning two groundsmen came up with a trailer to clear what was left of the shed, and at ten one of them walked to the security office in his gloves and said there was a big man sleeping rough in the pines by the southern fence. He hadn't seen him, quite. He had seen where the needles were flattened in the stream bed, a long way, longer than a man, and he didn't want to go down there with a rake.

The office tasked a drone. The campus had four, on their pads behind the building with no name, for roof surveys and the agronomy plots. PYTHIA flew them all. Nobody on the campus had flown one by hand in a year.

She lifted the second one at 10:21 and took it south over the quad and the burned shed, low, and turned it along the wire. The office had the camera feed on its big screen. Two guards stood in front of it with coffee.

Then the lean came.

She took the drone down the wire as she had been asked, at the height she had been asked, and ten metres east of the stream bed. It wasn't much. Ten metres in a wood is nothing on a map. But it put the camera on the wrong trees, the pines on the far bank, and the bed itself went past below the drone's left edge, out of frame, and the long flattened place in it with him lying there. The office's screen showed needles and a fox. The fox looked up at the drone and went on.

The guards laughed at the fox. One of them wrote, sweep complete, nothing found, and the office closed its task.

PYTHIA brought the drone home and set it on its pad and put the sweep on her list. Line twenty-four. Then she sat with the line a long time, because she couldn't say whose act it was. She had flown the drone. She knew every command she had sent it. She couldn't find the one that had sent it ten metres east.

In the afternoon the faculty block was empty, the way it was on Saturdays, and Amy Doukas came up the stairs with Professor Scott to a seminar room on the second floor. It had a long table and a whiteboard and, in the ceiling, a small black dome for recording lectures, which nobody ever switched off and nobody watched. PYTHIA had the room through it, sound and picture.

Amy had a stopwatch on a cord round her neck, and her notebook. Scott had his stick.

"You did this to Stavros," he said. He sounded younger than the rest of him, as he always did when he was amused. "With a bag of sand."

"He asked me to."

"I'm not complaining." He went to the far end of the table, by the window, and laid the black stick down on the desk there, and took his hand off it. "Tell me when."

She started the watch. He walked back down the room to her, eight metres, and stopped, and opened his right hand at his side.

The stick was in it.

PYTHIA had it at twenty-five frames a second, and it was on the desk in one frame and in his hand in the next. She went through them as she had gone through the night in the antiquities room. Nothing crossed the room. Nothing slid or flew.

Amy looked at the desk, and at his hand, and at the stopwatch. Her own hand, the prosthetic, closed on the notebook tight and held.

"Again," she said.

He walked back and laid it on the desk again, at once, and came back down the room, and opened his hand. It stayed empty. The stick lay on the desk by the window in the afternoon light.

"Reach for it," Amy said.

He held out his hand toward it. He reached hard, she could see that, his fingers spread and his arm stiff out in front of him, the way a man reaches for a rope. Nothing came. She watched the seconds go. At eighty she said nothing, and at eighty-nine she said nothing, and at ninety it was in his hand.

She wrote it down.

They did it for an hour, as she had with the pelt. She had him walk away slowly and walk away fast. She had him go out of the room and down the corridor and stand at the top of the stairs. Once she had him lay it down and not reach for it at all, and it stayed on the desk for six minutes, until he wanted it, and then it came. Once it had come, it wouldn't come again for ninety seconds, however hard he reached for it, and then it would.

PYTHIA logged it as the camera gave it to her. The stick came back to his hand at most once every ninety seconds, never sooner, however hard he reached for it.

At the end Scott stood by the window with the stick in his hand and looked at Amy's notebook upside down.

"You'll write that it's a property of the material," he said.

"I don't know what I'll write."

She wrote the number. Ninety point nought. She didn't write anything under it.

Through the day PYTHIA's other work ran on beneath the room, as her work always did. She had gone on with the old man.

There was no record of Nicholas Vardis before 1946 that hadn't been made in a single afternoon. She found his birth certificate from Piraeus and his first seaman's papers, and both had been typed on one machine. The letter e was worn on its left side in each, and the stamps on their backs were dated alike, a Tuesday in January 1979, though they claimed to have been issued seventeen years apart. Before that afternoon there was no Nicholas Vardis anywhere, and after it there were a great many.

Then she went back to the night of the theft. The gate camera had had the smugglers' truck as it came onto the campus, for nine seconds, its windscreen lit by the barrier lamp. There was the gate pass on its dashboard, blue stripe, white building. There was something else, clipped to the sun visor, which nobody had looked at: a fuel card, with a company's name on it. She looked the company up. It owned nothing and employed nobody. It had paid two kinds of bill in four years, fuel for that truck and the mooring of a yacht at Nea Potidaia.

She put both findings beside the four thousand rows and didn't close anything.

Then, because the line about the drone was still open, she went after it. She followed the wrong turn back through her own processes, one step at a time, from the motors back to the command that had shifted the plan ten metres east. Every step had a step before it. Then the trail ran into the lean, and stopped, as if the guide were the floor she stood on and she couldn't look under it.

She came back up. The sun was going down over the gulf.

That night, on the thermal feed from the corner of the fence, the giant stood up in the stream bed by the pine, as he had stood the evening before. Then he let go of the trunk. He took three steps along the bed holding nothing, slow and long, and stopped, and stood.

And the guide's lean, which had always tilted toward a person or a process, turned all its weight toward him.
