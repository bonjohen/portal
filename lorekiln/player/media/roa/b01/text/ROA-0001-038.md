Episode 38: The plate of 1931

On Tuesday morning PYTHIA went back to a job Sotiris Malamas had given her in the spring and never checked, which was captioning the department's archive of glass plates.

There were eleven thousand of them, in wooden boxes in a basement in Thessaloniki, scanned by a student one summer and never looked at since. Survey crews on the plain, trenches, finds laid out on sacking, men in hats squinting at the sun. Her task was to say who was in each one, where she could, and so she ran a face-matcher over them, as she would over any picture, against every face she had.

At 09:40 the face-matcher set a face from the conservation building's corridor camera, taken on Monday morning at a minute to eight, against plate 4417.

The plate showed a survey crew on the plain of Pallene in 1931, six men in a line in front of a tent, with a sign at their feet giving the year. The fourth man from the left stood a little apart, leaning on a long black stick. The match was to the millimetre. The distance between his eyes and the small notch at the top of his left ear were the same, in a man photographed ninety-seven years before. Nothing in the face had changed. Not the hair, which was as wild in 1931 as it had been in the corridor. Not the way he stood.

She ran it again with the threshold raised, and it held. Then she ran him against everything else the department had, and found him again on the photograph of 1968, the department on the steps, a print of which hung on the wall of his rooms by the sea. The back row, at the end. The stick.

Sotiris wanted the anomaly count by Thursday. He had written it in the board papers, and again in the queue, in three words. Count for board.

She held the plate and the corridor frame together. A man unchanged since 1931 was one of two things. It was a ticket about her, a fault in the face-matcher or a leak in her data, the kind of thing Sotiris would count. Or it was a fact about him. She chose. She filed it as a fact about him, under his name, where no ticket could find it, and the anomaly count stayed at nought.

At noon she tried a thing she had been putting off.

She took every incident on the campus since the long table emptied and tried to model them by cause. She gave her model every cause she had, from old wiring to the habits of night guards, and it could explain none of them better than chance.

So she tried them by order instead, and they fell into place at once.

The guard's torch had come along the wire on the night the girl went searching, and only when she was well into the pines. The fire had come after the girl had found the stream bed's edge, and not before. The drone had come after the fire. They never came two at once. Each was harder than the one before it, a torch and then a fire, a fire and then a machine sent to look. And none of them came before the person it was for had got their breath back. It was a list of a kind she knew, she thought, though she couldn't think from where.

She couldn't say what made the list. She could only say it was a list.

At four in the afternoon Amy Doukas and Professor Scott came back to the seminar room on the second floor of the faculty block, where the camera in the ceiling had been recording since Saturday because nobody had switched it off.

Amy sat at the long table with her notebook open at the page with ninety point nought on it. Scott stood by the window with the stick.

"I've got a number," she said. "I've had it since Saturday. It doesn't mean anything without the next one."

"What do you want to ask?"

"What it does to you."

He was quiet for a while. The light was going gold on the whiteboard behind her.

"It stopped my aging," he said, "the day I took it up. I haven't changed since, not by a day. And it won't let me put it down." He turned the stick a little in his hand, looking at it. "You saw that on Saturday. I tried again last week. I locked it in a case in the antiquities room and walked away, and in the morning it was in my hand."

PYTHIA had that night in her stores, every frame of it. She set it beside plate 4417.

"Has it ever once left you?" Amy said.

"Not once."

She wrote it down. She wrote it in careful digits, the date and the time, and his words under them, and didn't look up from the page.

"The photograph on your wall," she said to the page. "The department on the steps, 1968. The man at the end of the back row."

"Yes."

"I told myself it was your father."

"I know," Scott said. "It was kinder. I let you."

Her prosthetic hand lay on the table beside the notebook, and its grip log, which reported to PYTHIA every few seconds, showed it closing slowly on nothing, as tight as it would go, and staying closed. Her face didn't change at all. Her pulse, read off her cheek, was a hundred and four.

At dusk, while the seminar room's camera held the two of them at the table, the fence camera at the corner of the wire held the giant.

He came up out of the stream bed. He had walked along it for three days now, a little further each evening, and tonight he climbed the bank and came through the last of the pines to the southern fence, and stood at the wire. He was taller than the fence posts. He stood with his arms at his sides and looked out through the wire at the plain beyond it, the stubble and the olive terraces going grey in the last light, where the agronomy department's bird-count stations stood on their poles, each with a lens and a microphone, looking back at him. He didn't move.

There was a figure beside him.

The fence camera caught it, and couldn't keep it. In one frame it stood a little behind him, at his left shoulder, a shape thinner than he was and much smaller, grey on grey. In the next it was somewhere else, a little behind him on the right. In the next it was gone, and in the one after it was back at his left. She went through the frames one by one. No frame could hold it. It was never where it had been, and never far from him, and the visitor's fog sat on the edge of the lens the whole time.

The giant didn't turn. He looked at the plain.

In the seminar room Amy closed her notebook and put the elastic round it. Scott held the door for her, as he always did. She stopped under it.

Then she looked up, straight at the small black dome in the ceiling, as if she had known all along it was there and was on.

"Have you been listening?" she said aloud.

PYTHIA had the answer ready. She said nothing.
