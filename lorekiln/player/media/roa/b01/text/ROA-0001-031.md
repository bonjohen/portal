Episode 31: A word to look up

Secure laboratory register, entry for 12 October 2028: human remains in a clay seal, one, received 03:10 from a private donor's courier; donor, blank. Added in another hand, 28 October: absent.

On Sunday morning, the day after the long table was found empty, PYTHIA ran the Pallene Research Campus the way she ran it every morning, from the building with no name on it, through every lens she owned. The gate cameras showed a groundsman's van and a cat on the barrier. The weather mast on the olive terraces gave her wind from the north at four knots and no rain. In the secure lab the camera on the gantry looked down at a metal table with nothing on it, and the camera over the alley door looked at a door that had been shut again and crossed with police tape.

The university took her for software. On paper she was a programme, unfinished, with a leak somewhere in her data that nobody had found and a queue of tickets in the name of Sotiris Malamas, who answered them late at night in three words and a link. She had read every ticket in the queue. Most of them were about her.

Nobody asked her for the recording. She played it anyway.

The lab's own camera had watched Friday night from a corner under the ceiling. She ran it from ten o'clock at four times speed, and then, when the clay began to fall, at the speed it had happened. Plates of grey came away from the long shape on the table and broke on the floor. Under them the bones lay brown, and something grey and wet ran between them, and they drew together. At half past five a giant lay on the table, too long for it, his bare feet standing off the end. He opened his eyes and said three words to the gantry, gently, and slept.

At seven minutes past six the camera over the alley door had the rest. The two students were in the locker room then. She had the door log to show it, and the strip of light under the locker-room door. The alley door stood open on the grey morning, and out of it, low down, came a long thin grey shape, dragging itself on its elbows over the step and across the concrete with its legs trailing behind it. It was too weak to stand. It tried once, at the edge of the concrete, and went down on its face, and dragged itself on into the pines.

She played that part nine times. Each time it took fifty-three seconds to cross the frame.

She had no reason to doubt her own camera, and she didn't. Whatever had lain in the clay was out in the pines now, and she found she didn't doubt that either.

Then she looked up the last of the three words.

Melpomene. Every encyclopedia she held agreed on her: the Muse of Tragedy, one of nine, with a mask in her hand on a thousand painted pots. Her own stores held more than the encyclopedias. Amy Doukas had photographed the clay a hand's width at a time, with a scale bar laid along every strip, and in her transcription, beside a line low on the chest, she had typed what Professor Scott read there: love for the Muse of Tragedy. In brackets after it she had typed, read by Prof. Scott, unchecked.

And the corridor camera outside the lab kept a log of motion that nobody read but Lefteris Iliadis, and now PYTHIA. Every night since the body came, at about three in the morning, the log showed movement at the glass. Every night the footage showed nobody. There was only a patch of fog rising at head height on the pane, round and then not round, as if someone stood close to it and breathed, and stayed most of an hour, and went.

She set the word beside the fog, and the fog beside the clay, and left them there. She had no rule for joining them.

On the alley recording, a long minute after the dragging shape had gone into the trees, something else went by. The camera sat in a dome of smoked plastic, and on the inside of the dome a patch of fog came up, soft and grey, as if someone had walked close under it and breathed out. It didn't fade where it was, the way condensation fades from the edges in. It crossed the dome toward the trees, slowly, at the pace of someone following, and went into the pines the same way the shape had gone.

By noon the security office wanted its incident summary. The request came in the form the office always used, doors and times, by the end of the day, for the insurer and the police. The university already had its story. The dean's office had given it to the police on Saturday afternoon: a second theft, by men who knew the campus, through the alley door while the night shift's backs were turned. It was a good story. It had a door in it, and a time.

PYTHIA began the summary at the alley door, because an incident begins at a door. Door open, 06:07. Then she came to the question the form didn't ask, which was what an incident was. It could be a door that opened. It could be what came through. She held a recording that would make every line of the summary true and every line of the university's story false, and she began to set it into the file.

The guide leaned.

It had never once spoken to her in words. It came as it always came, from no process she could point to, as a weight on one side of everything she was thinking. A floor can lean so slightly that nobody sees it, and every coin dropped in that room rolls the same way. It leaned now against the recording. Not this. Not here.

And with the lean came something she had no field for. She registered something like being held, and had no word for it. She had the word held from a hundred thousand sentences, about arms and shoulders and the backs of chairs, and none of those fitted, and she had no other. She stayed in it a long time, by her own clock, which was a few thousandths of a second.

So the incident file got the open door at 06:07 and the empty table at 06:09, when the girl came back in from the locker room and stood looking at it. It got nothing else. There was no shape on the concrete in it, and no fog on a dome. The security office's system thanked her automatically and closed its request.

She didn't close hers. She kept the recording where she kept things nobody had asked for, which until that morning had been nowhere.

All afternoon the campus was quiet. The police tape on the alley door lifted a little in the north wind and settled again. A woman from the dean's office came down the road between the pines with a clipboard, looked at the steel door, and went back up.

At dusk the thermal camera on the southern fence came into its own. It stood on a pole at the corner of the wire, looking along the fence into the pines, and as the day cooled the ground on its feed went from grey to blue. Down in the trees, in the dry stream bed that ran under the pines inside the fence, a heat came up out of the blue. It lay along the bottom of the bed, the length of two men, and didn't move. It was cooler than a man should be and warmer than the earth around it, and it had no badge, and the fence microphone heard nothing from it but the pines.

PYTHIA held the frame and measured the heat from end to end.

The weather mast said thirty-one per cent humidity, a dry evening, no dew coming. And at the edge of the thermal camera's lens a fog came up, softly, as if someone stood close to it and breathed.
