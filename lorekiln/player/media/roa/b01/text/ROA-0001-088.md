Episode 88: Blood on the grass

On Thursday afternoon Rigas came back to the parcel with rifles, as he had said he would.

Professor Scott was in the conservation store when he heard. A student put her head round the door, white in the face, and said there were guards at the lab with guns, real ones, and the giant wouldn't move. Zoe was at her bench with the apple's report. She looked up at Scott and didn't tell him to stay, and he was grateful for that afterwards.

He went down the road behind the conservation building as fast as his stick would let him, which was faster than he let anyone see.

The secure lab stood at the bottom of the road with its one row of high windows, and a stone's throw behind it, past the last of the pines, ran the old southern fence of the campus, which was the edge of the parcel. In the autumn the giant had walked out through that fence when he first stood, and the gap he had made was still there, two posts leant apart and the wire hanging between them, with the scrub going away beyond it toward the coast road. The line on Sofia's map ran exactly there. Inside the wire the grass was the university's. Outside it was only Pallene.

The giant was sitting on the grass under the windows, where he had sat most of the week. Six of the security firm's guards stood in a half circle on the path in front of him, and four of them had rifles, held across their chests the way men hold things they have been trained to hold and never used. Rigas stood in the middle in his black fleece with a sheet of paper in his hand.

"Off campus," Rigas was saying. "That's the order. You walk out the main gate, and you keep walking, and nobody gets hurt."

"I live here," the giant said, in the modern speech. "I have a paper."

"The paper's being looked at."

"Then look at it," said the giant, "and leave me to sit."

The long cut on his cheek from Monday was still open. It had stopped bleeding but it hadn't closed, a dark line along the bone with the edges gone pale, and Scott, coming down the last of the road, saw one of the guards looking at it as he had looked at it on Monday.

The giant stood up.

He didn't do it to frighten them, Scott thought later. He did it because a man stands up when he is told to leave his own house. But he was eight feet of grey bone and shadow coming up off the grass all at once, and one of the guards on the end had never seen him stand before, and that guard fired.

The shot was very loud between the buildings. The giant's left arm jerked, and he looked down at it. Then a second guard fired, and the giant turned half round with the round in his side, under the ribs, and took a step back.

It was a step back toward the fence. It took him to the gap in the wire.

"Hold your fire!" Scott was shouting. He didn't remember starting. "Hold your fire, you fools!"

The giant took one more step, through the gap, between the leaning posts, and stood on the scrub on the far side of the line with one hand pressed to his side. Blood ran out between his fingers and down his arm and dropped from his elbow onto the dry grass and the pine needles.

Scott reached the fence. He was between the rifles and the gap, and he didn't know how he had got there. Rigas was shouting at his men to lower their weapons, and at Scott to get out of the way, both at once. Scott didn't hear him. His hand was so tight on the black stick that he could feel the grain of it through his palm, and his anger had come up into his arm from wherever it lived, and he made himself look at the ground.

He saw the giant's blood on the grass beyond the parcel line draw back into the soil.

It went slowly, the way water goes into dry earth after the first rain, but it was not soaking in. It was going home. He watched a dark drop on a blade of grass shrink to nothing, and the blade stand clean. He watched the stain on the pine needles thin and go. And above it, on the giant's arm, the wound where the first round had gone in drew itself shut, the edges coming together like lips, until there was only a pale mark on the grey skin, and then not that.

Then Scott looked down at his own feet, inside the wire.

The first blood had fallen there, on the parcel side, when the first round struck and before the giant stepped back. It lay on the university's grass in dark drops, red on green, just as it had fallen. It didn't move. It wasn't going anywhere at all.

Behind him Rigas had got his men's rifles pointed at the ground. Nobody was firing.

"He stood up at my men," Rigas said. He had come to Scott's shoulder, breathing hard. "You saw it. He went for them."

Scott turned and looked at him. He didn't say anything. He found he couldn't trust himself with a single word, because every word he had was in his arm, and his arm was on the stick. He looked at the man until Rigas stepped back, and then he put the stick behind him, in both hands, against the small of his back, the way he had once seen a priest hold a staff he didn't want to strike with.

Scott turned to the giant across the wire and spoke in the old tongue, low, so that the guards would understand none of it.

"On the parcel you bleed," he said. "You bleed, and you stay bled. Here, on that grass. I told you in the dean's office and you laughed. I don't know how much of that even you can lose, and neither do you. Look."

The giant looked.

He looked at the grass on his side of the wire, clean, with the side wound already closing under his hand. He looked at Scott's side, at the red drops lying where they had fallen on the university's grass. He touched the open cut on his cheek, Monday's cut, which had not healed in three days. For a long time he looked from one kind of blood to the other and said nothing.

"Come away," Scott said. "Walk south. Go and sit in the theatre. Anywhere but here."

The giant raised his eyes from the grass to the lab's row of high windows, the corridor behind them, the glass where once, every night at three, someone had stood looking in for him. Then he stepped back through the gap in the wire onto the parcel.

The guards lifted their rifles. Rigas shouted, and they lowered them again.

Alcyoneus walked past Scott without looking at him, across the grass to the wall under the windows, and sat down where he had been sitting, with his back to the bricks and his long legs out across the path, and folded his hands in his lap.

Nobody moved him. After a while the guards went away up the road, Rigas last, looking back.

Scott stayed. He sat on the step of the lab with the stick across his knees until it was dark, and the giant didn't speak, and neither did he.

Clio didn't come to the steps of the lecture block that night. At ten Scott's phone lit on his knee. It was Amy.

She's not on the steps. I've been to the pines and the archive roof. I can't find her anywhere.
